Teaching Effectiveness & Student Outcomes

My course evaluations across technical higher-education subjects consistently demonstrate strong student learning gains, high engagement, and well-structured instruction—particularly in online and hybrid delivery formats. These results reflect a teaching approach focused on professional skill development, applied learning, and clear, responsive communication.

✔ Development of Professional Skills

  • In TEE 1200 (Spring 2022), 100% of students rated progress on developing professional skills as “Substantial” or “Exceptional.”
  • In TEE 1200 (Fall 2022), skill-development scores were in the “Higher” bucket compared to institutional and discipline norms.

✔ Mastery of Core Technical Content

  • In TEE 1200 (Spring 2022), 100% of students rated understanding of core subject matter as “Substantial” or “Exceptional.”
  • Students highlighted strong alignment to real-world engineering and design applications.

✔ Application, Problem-Solving & Project-Based Learning

  • TESY 3020 (Summer 2022): 100% of students rated progress in applying course material to problem-solving as “Substantial” or “Exceptional.”
  • Students praised hands-on project freedom, industry-relevant assignments, and portfolio-ready work.
  • In TESY 4330 (Innovation), students showed excellent progress in developing creativity and design thinking.

✔ Leadership in Online & Hybrid Instruction

  • In TEE 1200 (Fall 2022), 85.7% of students agreed the instructor’s use of technology improved their learning.
  • Students praised “outstanding” online lectures that felt like “one-on-one instruction.”
  • Course organization was consistently rated as clear, structured, and easy to follow.

✔ High-Touch Mentorship & Student Support

  • Across multiple courses, students described my feedback as “quick,” “helpful,” and “always available.”
  • 100% of TESY 3020 students agreed the instructor was highly engaged and responded in a timely manner.
  • Qualitative feedback consistently mentions care, guidance, flexibility, and going “above and beyond.”

These data points come from independent course evaluations as a part of the university institutional research and evaluation process.

Curriculum Samples & Instructional Design Showcase

These curriculum samples highlight my approach to designing high-quality technical, project-based, and professional-skills courses across higher education and CTE programs. The examples below draw from Canvas modules, project briefs, lab structures, and major assignments used in courses I developed and taught.

✔ Comprehensive Curriculum Design & Module Structure

I design courses using a clear scope-and-sequence approach that builds skills progressively and aligns instruction with industry requirements.

  • TEE 1200 — 3D Modeling & Solidworks
    Curriculum progresses from foundational sketches and material properties to advanced modeling, multi-view drawings, and certification prep for the CSWA Exam.
  • TEE 2300 — Electronic Fundamentals
    Module sequence moves from lab safety and basic circuits to AC/DC power, PWM, relays, and digital electronics (555 Timer, logic gates), highlighting structured technical lab design.
  • TEE 3050 — Media, Graphics & Technology
    Interdisciplinary modules integrate hardware, copyright & licensing, photography, lighting, Photoshop, audio, video, and Illustrator — demonstrating modern media curriculum planning.
  • TESY 3020 — Professional & Workforce Skills
    Week-by-week curriculum focuses on ethics, creativity, communication, leadership, teamwork, decision-making, and project planning.
  • TESY 4330 — Design Thinking
    Curriculum covers design thinking history, empathy, user needs, customer response, storytelling, prototyping, and evaluation.

✔ High-Impact Projects & Assessment Design

My courses use large, integrated projects that measure applied technical skill, creativity, and professional readiness.

  • TEE 3050 Final Project — Multidisciplinary Media & Product Design
    Students design and laser-cut a working toy product (10+ assembled pieces, 5+ moving parts), and produce: flyer/poster, 30-second radio commercial, 30-second video commercial, and a 5–7 minute presentation.
  • TEE 1200 Final Project — Engineering Design & Documentation
    Students model and document a 10-part mechanical assembly using Solidworks, producing part drawings, exploded views, BOM, mass/COM data, and a photo-real rendering.
  • TESY 3020 Final Project — Project Lifecycle Management
    Sequence includes pitch, proposal drafts, progress reports, and a final presentation — demonstrating strong project planning and professional communication skills.

✔ Instructional Rigor & Technical Reporting Standards

  • TEE 2300 Lab Reports: Emphasize detailed procedures, schematics, prototype photos, materials lists, troubleshooting analysis, and reflective conclusions.
  • TESY 4330 Design Thinking Tools: Students create empathy maps, personas, design briefs, customer experience maps, and prototype evaluations.
  • Ethics & Academic Integrity modules implemented across courses demonstrate strong alignment with institutional and professional expectations.

These curriculum samples demonstrate my ability to design modern, relevant, industry-aligned learning experiences that integrate technical mastery, creativity, and professional competencies.
